arXiv - 22 papers
Internalising the Identity Primitive: Cryptographic Individuality for an Autonomous Agent on a Public Blockchain
arXiv 2608.02986 · Aug 4, 2026 · Keisuke Suzuki
Asks what makes an autonomous agent a genuine "individual" once it holds economic stakes and transacting authority, and answers by binding the agent's identity to its private key cryptographically rather than to hardware or operator trust - deployed live on Solana devnet, with a protocol-imposed "metabolic cost" debited each cycle from a key-derived account. Identity infrastructure underneath the authorization question, adjacent to what FACT and ATLAS need answered: which agent instance actually did this.
MMShopBench: A Real-Log Benchmark for Multimodal, Multi-Turn Shopping Agents
arXiv 2607.29002 · Jul 31, 2026
First real-log (not synthetic) benchmark for shopping agents: 289 cases built from cleaned and manually annotated logs of an actual AI shopping assistant, each one requiring the agent to interleave image and text understanding across a multi-turn conversation to ground purchase intent and mandatory product attributes. Complements the largely text-only benchmarks already on this page with genuine multimodal, real-world shopping requests.
MerchantBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Long-Term Coherence in E-Commerce Operations
arXiv 2607.28956 · Jul 31, 2026 · Shi, Tao, Jin, Kang, Dou, Zhu, Pan, Fu, Wang, Li, Cheng, Weng, Huo
A 365-day, order-level simulation grounded in 98,843 real product records and 26 tools, testing LLM agents as autonomous sellers handling sourcing, pricing, cash flow and delayed order outcomes over an extended horizon rather than a single transaction. The best LLM configuration reached only 27.3% of the net assets a human seller achieved - a concrete gap in agents' capacity to stay purposeful over time, not just competent at a single decision.
SAGE: An LLM-driven Self Reflective Agentic Framework for Fraud Detection
arXiv 2606.08146 · Jun 6, 2026 · Chen, Li, Liang, Wang, Liu
First end-to-end multi-agent LLM framework for fraud detection explicitly targeting payment and e-commerce fraud: three coordinated agents work through a six-layer Data Diagnostic Tree, guided by a natural-language-gradient decision process. Reports winning 96% of method-dataset comparisons and a 40.9% average F1 improvement over baselines across five fraud datasets - a stronger, more explicitly agentic entrant in the same space as this page's existing "Toward Auditable Fraud Detection" card, which found a plain classifier beating an investigation agent.
Agentic Commerce World: An Auditable and Verifiable Environment for Vibe Commerce
arXiv 2608.02441 · Aug 3, 2026 · Fan, Yang, Wang, Chen, Zhang, Wei, Li, McAuley, Zhang, Yu, Yu, Liu
Introduces an environment for AI agents to run marketplace transactions with separated buyer/merchant responsibilities and privacy, validated through a Vibe Commerce Protocol that checks each agent action before committing it to shared transaction state. Evaluates 260 benchmark tasks (scores 56.1%-91.4% across models) and finds that scoring the transaction process, not just the final state, is what actually separates good agents from lucky ones.
Do Humans Bargain Differently with AI? Evidence from Alternating-Offer Games
arXiv 2608.01212 · Aug 2, 2026
Lab experiment finds human proposers systematically offer AI counterparties less than they offer human counterparties, while human responders are significantly more willing to accept unfair, below-market offers from an AI than from a human - direct behavioral evidence that people apply a different fairness norm to AI negotiating agents, relevant to how autonomous purchasing/negotiation agents should present offers to stay perceived as fair.
Can LLM Agents Price Competitively? A Dynamic Multi-Attribute Auction Benchmark for Agentic Commerce
arXiv 2608.00102 · Jul 30, 2026 · Ahmed, Cai, Minaei, Rachuri
Introduces Bazaar, a dynamic sealed-bid benchmark with closed-form customer utilities, so agent pricing can be scored exactly against the optimum instead of judged qualitatively - opaque customer preferences, dynamically responding competitors and fluctuating demand. Across 11 frontier models from four providers, the leaders on customer acquisition are not the leaders on profit, and rankings reorder under demand shocks, with the fastest-learning agents slowest to readjust afterwards. Headline result: even the strongest agent captures under a third of hindsight-optimal profit, a concrete ceiling on autonomous pricing as it stands.
FAVA: Formal Authorization for Verified Agents with Evidence-Backed Permission Graphs
arXiv 2607.27267 · Jul 29, 2026 · Zhang, Zhao, Liu, Lou, Cheng, Liu
Uses an LLM to translate a natural-language agent task into a formal Permission Intermediate Representation, then a deterministic lowering pass builds an evidence-backed permission graph tracking data flows and dependencies for runtime authorization decisions - a concrete answer to "what exactly did the user authorize this agent to do."
When HTTP 402 Meets the Blockchain: Risks on Emerging x402 Payments
arXiv 2607.19545 · Jul 21, 2026 · Wang, Yang, Chen, Ji, Payer · accepted to USENIX Security 2026
The first systematic security study of x402's facilitator-mediated deployments in the wild, identifying four new attack classes: Free Shopping, Asset Theft, Service Denial and Gas Abuse. Notable for its USENIX Security acceptance, a strong peer-review signal rare among the arXiv preprints on this page.
Protocol-Level Attacks on Agentic Commerce Platforms: A Cross-Platform Taxonomy, AIP-Bench, and Unified Defense
arXiv 2607.21824 · Jul 23, 2026
Finds 33 deterministic vulnerabilities across three leading agentic commerce platforms, each succeeding regardless of the underlying model, with three chaining into complete payment hijacking - a 100% structural attack-success rate. Introduces AIP-Bench to test for these and PCAT, a defense that drives four of five structural attack classes to zero without platform changes. The clearest evidence yet that protocol-layer security, not just model alignment, is the gap agentic commerce needs to close.
Toward Cryptographically Verifiable Authorization for Autonomous AI Agents
arXiv 2607.21325 · Jul 23, 2026
Formalizes agent authorization as a relation binding agent principal, request and execution context, using zero-knowledge proofs to protect confidential attributes - a proof-of-concept implementation via zk-SNARKs. Directly the kind of primitive FACT and ATLAS would sit on top of.
Building Trust in Autonomous Commerce: A Verifiable Global Event Timeline and AI-Ready Fraud Intelligence Layer
arXiv 2607.19436 · Jul 21, 2026
Canonical event schemas, deterministic batch formation and Merkle-based commitments anchored to a blockchain, plus a cryptographically signed fraud-marker system - claims 50,000 events processed in 47ms with logarithmic proof sizes. A tamper-evident record layer for agentic transactions, adjacent to what RAILS and TessPay target from the clearing side.
Toward Auditable Fraud Detection: Combining Graph Features, Model Explanations, and Agentic Case Investigation
arXiv 2607.19266 · Jul 21, 2026
Tests an LLM investigation agent given model explanations, graph context and retrieved reference cases against plain classifier thresholding on uncertain transactions - the agent scored worse (65.0% vs 71.7% accuracy), and of the 8 decisions it changed, 6 replaced correct calls with errors. Core finding: "a plausible rationale from an investigation agent is not evidence of a better decision," a caution for any agentic fraud-review layer sold on explainability alone.
SovereignNegotiation-Bench: Evaluating User-Owned Personal Agents in Delegated Bargaining
arXiv 2607.02814 · Jul 2, 2026 · Dylan Zongmin Liu, Stanford
A trace-level benchmark for personal agents negotiating on a user's behalf (splitting costs, appealing decisions, requesting refunds) - scores agreement success jointly with user utility, privacy, consent, evidence grounding and auditability, since an agent can "win" a negotiation while still harming the user it represents.

Agentic Proxies: Governance, Accountability, and the Architecture of a Trustworthy AI Economy
SSRN 6952119 · Geoff Lundholm · working draft, Jun 2026
Argues the real accountability gap in agentic AI is AI-to-AI delegation, not human-to-AI interaction, and proposes "bounded delegation" - agents acting under explicit, verifiable, traceable authority as "agentic proxies" - as structurally more insurable than fully autonomous agents, since insurers need identifiable exposure, attributable causation and verifiable governance before they'll underwrite it.
